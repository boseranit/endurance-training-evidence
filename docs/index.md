# Endurance Training Evidence

Research-to-practice reference, with a cycling focus.

A maintained research-to-practice reference for endurance training. It records **what improves VO₂max and sustainable cycling performance, for whom, at what recovery cost, and with what uncertainty**. The practical scope is a trained amateur with approximately **7–12 total training hours per week**, not a professional training 30 hours. Look up a question, topic, graded conclusion or source; do not treat this as a book to read in order.

**Evidence checked: 2026-10-02.** This is a targeted, critically appraised investigation, not an exhaustive systematic review. [Methods and update rules](research-methods.md) explain the access limits and evidence grades. That date is not a new review.

## What is authoritative?

The **topic page's conclusion** is the maintained judgment. The **source record** is the traceable extraction. The **model topic** connects the mechanisms and priorities; the **program-design topic** applies them without declaring its exact examples optimal. This front page and the conclusion register route to those documents, not independent competing advice.

New findings should revise an existing conclusion when they address the same question. Keep stable claim IDs, document conflicting evidence, update affected applications, and record the change. See [the update procedure](research-methods.md).

## How to ask the next question

Examples:

- “Using the maintained evidence, should I add 90 minutes of easy riding or a third hard session at nine hours per week?”
- “Which conclusion supports 30/15 intervals, and did the study measure acute oxygen uptake or long-term performance?”
- “This new paper compares polarized and pyramidal training. Update the relevant conclusion, grade, and practical implication.”
- “Which recommendation changes if my event is a 20-minute time trial rather than a five-hour road race?”

Answers should link the relevant conclusion and original study, identify the studied population, and separate a supported principle from a coaching choice. An apparently precise prescription is not stronger evidence simply because it contains exact watts or percentages.

## Boundaries
