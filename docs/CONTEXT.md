# Cycling-training evidence context

Preferred terminology for this platform. Definitions keep unlike measurements from being treated as synonyms. For evidence and application, use the [topic index](index.md).

## Performance and physiological boundaries

**Maximal oxygen uptake (VO₂max):** The greatest rate of whole-body oxygen uptake demonstrated during an appropriate maximal exercise test. Absolute uptake and body-mass-relative uptake answer different performance questions.

**Fractional utilization:** The fraction of VO₂max sustained during a specified task or at a specified physiological boundary. It is duration- and protocol-dependent, not one fixed percentage for an athlete.

**First lactate or ventilatory threshold (LT1 / VT1):** Operational estimates of the transition from the moderate to the heavy exercise-intensity domain. Lactate and ventilatory methods may disagree; a fixed blood-lactate concentration is not a universal individual threshold.

**Second lactate or ventilatory threshold (LT2 / VT2):** Method-dependent estimates of a higher-intensity physiological transition. They are not automatically interchangeable with critical power, maximal lactate steady state, or FTP.

**Critical power (CP):** The asymptote of an appropriately fitted power–duration relationship, used as an estimate of the heavy–severe intensity-domain boundary. Test selection, fitting method, and measurement error affect the estimate.

**Maximal lactate steady state (MLSS):** The highest tested constant workload meeting a stated blood-lactate stability criterion over a specified test duration. It is an operational laboratory measurement, not a synonym for CP.

**Functional threshold power (FTP):** A cycling performance estimate defined by the test or software used. A percentage of a 20-minute effort, a ramp-test estimate, and an actual sustained performance are different measurements.
_Avoid_: using “FTP,” “LT2,” “CP,” and “MLSS” interchangeably.

**W′:** The curvature constant in a CP power–duration model, with units of work. It is not a direct measurement of a single anaerobic energy store, and estimates depend on the model and testing conditions.

**Gross efficiency:** External mechanical work divided by total metabolic energy expenditure during a specified workload. “Economy” denotes the metabolic cost of producing a specified output; the two measures are related but not identical.

**Durability:** The magnitude and timing of deterioration in physiological or performance characteristics during prolonged exercise. A fresh power–duration curve does not measure durability.

## Training classification

**Low-intensity endurance riding:** Riding predominantly below the athlete's estimated LT1 / VT1, with sustainable breathing and low perceived exertion. It is research-model Zone 1 and often cycling-model Zone 2, but the mapping is not exact.
_Avoid_: “Zone 2” without naming the zone model.

**Three-zone research model:** Zone 1 below LT1 / VT1; Zone 2 between first and second thresholds; Zone 3 above the second threshold. The chosen threshold methods must be stated.

**Cycling power-zone model:** A convention dividing power into five, six, or seven zones, commonly using percentages of FTP. Its numbered zones do not necessarily coincide with an athlete's physiological boundaries.

**Training-intensity distribution:** The allocation of training across defined intensity categories. Session-goal classification, time-in-zone, and distance-based classification are different counting methods.

**Hard cycling session:** A session with a substantial deliberate high-intensity, threshold, or race stimulus and a meaningful recovery cost. “Two hard days” does not mean two VO₂ sessions plus additional threshold workouts and races.

**VO₂-focused intervals:** Repeated work intended to stimulate maximal aerobic capacity and/or performance in the severe domain. Neither a fixed percentage of FTP nor breathlessness demonstrates that VO₂max was reached.

**Sweet-spot training:** A coaching category describing sustained work somewhat below an estimated FTP. It is not a uniquely validated physiological intensity domain or a proven universal stimulus-to-fatigue optimum.

## Interpretation and recovery

**Acute response:** A physiological or performance response during or shortly after one exposure. Acute signaling or time near VO₂max is not proof of a subsequent training adaptation.

**Longitudinal adaptation:** A persistent change measured after repeated training exposures. A controlled intervention is needed to attribute a difference to a particular training prescription with confidence.

**Evidence grade:** A qualitative assessment of support for a precise conclusion in the relevant population. This platform uses Strong evidence, Moderate evidence, Plausible but uncertain, and Mostly coaching practice; these are not numerical scores or formal GRADE ratings.

**Energy availability:** Dietary energy remaining after exercise expenditure, considered relative to fat-free mass. Estimates are uncertain, and one calculated daily value or universal cutoff cannot diagnose relative energy deficiency in sport.

**Relative energy deficiency in sport (RED-S):** A clinical syndrome of impaired health and performance associated with problematic low energy availability. Assessment is individualized and extends beyond body weight or a single laboratory marker.

**Plasma volume:** The fluid component of circulating blood. Expansion can lower measured haemoglobin concentration without reducing total haemoglobin mass.

**Haemoglobin mass:** The total amount of circulating haemoglobin, distinct from its concentration in a blood sample. Plasma expansion alone does not demonstrate increased red-cell or haemoglobin mass.

**Training-load model:** A calculation summarizing selected training inputs, such as power, heart rate, or session RPE. Model outputs such as CTL are not direct measurements of fitness, recovery, or injury risk.
