# Designing a recoverable program

**Evidence checked: 2026-10-02.** This chapter applies the preceding evidence to a healthy, already-trained cyclist with **7–12 total available training hours/week**, including strength. It is not an individualized medical prescription or an experimentally validated optimal package.

## Programming principles for 7–12 hours

| Principle | Actionable starting point | Evidence grade and boundary |
|---|---|---|
| **Weekly volume** | Start from tolerated volume; add mostly easy time when quality, energy and recovery remain intact | **Moderate evidence** for dose/context; exact hours/ramp **Mostly coaching practice** |
| **Hard sessions** | Usually one or two hard cycling exposures; an initial mixed week uses one VO₂-oriented and one sustained session | **Moderate evidence** that studied doses work; optimal two-day rule **Mostly coaching practice** |
| **Endurance intensity** | Conversational, ~RPE 2–3/10, intended below LT1; power/HR cross-check, not rigid FTP conversion | **Moderate evidence** for individualized domains; exact percentage **Mostly coaching practice** |
| **VO₂ work** | Begin e.g. 4×4 (16 work minutes); build toward 20–25, or a tolerated alternative such as 4×8 / sustained 30/15 series | **Moderate evidence** for formats; precise progression/dose **Mostly coaching practice**; work minutes ≠ near-max oxygen minutes |
| **Threshold work** | Begin ~24–30 accumulated minutes, progress toward ~40–45 when controlled; use recent protocol-specific FTP only as a guide | **Moderate evidence** for sustained work; exact recipe/weekly amount **Mostly coaching practice** |
| **Long rides** | Approximately 2–3.5 h in these examples, primarily easy/fueled; extend only for tolerance/event need | **Moderate evidence** for accumulated-work effects; duration/share **Mostly coaching practice** |
| **Strength** | Familiar progressive heavy work; often two sessions for building, one for maintenance; count time/stress | **Moderate evidence**, limited certainty; abbreviated 30-minute template **Mostly coaching practice** |
| **Recovery days** | Rest or short genuinely easy riding; begin with ~48 h between hard cycling days; longer if needed | Precise spacing **Mostly coaching practice**; active-recovery superiority unproved |
| **Deloads** | Periodic reduced-load opportunity, triggered earlier by persistent fatigue; reduce duration/hard work, do not compensate later | Fixed 3:1 calendar **Mostly coaching practice**; taper evidence does not prove routine deload optimum |
| **Progression** | Change one main load lever at a time when feasible; hold after increases; use execution/recovery rather than “10% is safe” | Broad overload/adaptation **Moderate evidence**; exact steps **Mostly coaching practice** |
| **Fueling** | Match daily energy/carbohydrate to work; practice ~60–90 g/h on long demanding rides; rapid-recovery intake only when needed | Carbohydrate versus none **Strong evidence**; dose application **Moderate evidence**; >100 superiority **Plausible but uncertain** |
| **Sleep** | Protect regular adequate opportunity; extend a short habitual pattern before adding stress; naps optional with wake-up buffer | Severe-loss harm **Strong evidence**; extension **Moderate evidence**; exact nine-hours-plus-nap law unproved |

**Compared with a 30-hour professional:** retain the principles, not the total exposure. Extra professional hours are largely additional easy work, not proportionately more VO₂ sessions. Their longer rides, high energy intake, naps and support/tolerance do not fit automatically around an amateur job. The lower total-time budget means strength, long rides and two quality sessions compete for the same hours. Heat/activation refinements should not displace the core work or sleep.

## Worked 7-, 9- and 12-hour weeks

### Conventions and assumptions

- **Hours are total training**, cycling **plus strength**. If the stated hours mean cycling-only, lifting adds time; do not hide it outside a genuine availability ceiling.
- Cycling durations include warm-up, between-bout recoveries and easy finish/cool-down. Daily totals below exclude travel, changing and meal preparation.
- **Easy:** conversational RPE 2–3/10; ~55–70% of a recent protocol-defined FTP is only an initial communication range, adjusted to LT1/VT1 and response. Recovery spins can be easier.
- **Sustained/threshold-oriented:** controlled hard RPE ~6–7/10, often around 90–100% of that FTP estimate; lower it if this is not repeatable. This is not proof of MLSS/CP.
- **VO₂-oriented:** highest repeatable whole-set power, late-bout RPE often ~8–9/10; no mandatory %FTP or claim of measured VO₂max. The published Borg 6–20 values are a different scale.
- Strength assumes familiar technique/load and counts the warm-up. For a new lifter, learn technique and reduce cycling stress rather than jumping to heavy loads.
- Monday rest follows the weekend. Tuesday/Thursday quality gives roughly 48 h. Strength after cycling protects cycling priority; separate by several hours if feasible, but do not claim a universal required gap. Sunday's second session is familiar/low-volume; omit or move it if it impairs Tuesday.
- These are illustrative ordinary weeks—not maximal-load, race or five-day HIT-block weeks. A race replaces a quality session and its associated time.

<a id="seven-hour-week"></a>

### 7-hour week

| Day | Cycling | Strength | Objective and approximate intensity / structure | Placement rationale |
|---|---:|---:|---|---|
| Mon | 0 min | 0 | Rest | Recover from weekend; no obligatory recovery spin |
| Tue | 65 min | 30 min | VO₂-oriented: 15 warm-up + **4×4** + **3×3** easy recovery + 25 easy finish; familiar strength afterwards | Fresh quality; consolidate some stress before easy/rest days |
| Wed | 55 min | 0 | Easy endurance, RPE 2–3 | Adds recoverable exposure without another hard session |
| Thu | 60 min | 0 | Sustained work: 15 warm-up + **2×12** + **5** easy between + 16 easy finish | Second hard cycling exposure, ~48 h after Tuesday |
| Fri | 0 min | 0 | Rest | Protect weekend tolerance; unused time is not a problem |
| Sat | 120 min | 0 | Fueled long easy ride, mostly below LT1 | Continuous exposure; ~31% of cycling minutes, not a proven optimum |
| Sun | 90 min | 0 | Easy endurance; shorten if Saturday was costly | Adds time only if it remains low cost |
| **Total** | **390 min / 6 h 30** | **30 min** | **420 min / 7 h** | Two hard cycling days; one strength session |

One strength session is a budget compromise, particularly appropriate for maintenance. A two-session strength-development priority requires replacing cycling time or another stressor, not adding an unseen hour.

### 9-hour week

| Day | Cycling | Strength | Objective and approximate intensity / structure | Placement rationale |
|---|---:|---:|---|---|
| Mon | 0 min | 0 | Rest | Weekend recovery |
| Tue | 75 min | 30 min | VO₂-oriented: 15 warm-up + **5×4** + **4×3** easy + 28 easy finish; strength later | Primary fresh quality |
| Wed | 60 min | 0 | Easy endurance, RPE 2–3 | Low-cost exposure between quality days |
| Thu | 75 min | 0 | Sustained work: 15 warm-up + **3×12** + **2×5** easy + 14 easy finish | 36 controlled work minutes; ~48 h spacing |
| Fri | 45 min | 0 | Short easy/recovery ride, or rest if needed | Not a third workout; preserves Saturday |
| Sat | 180 min | 0 | Fueled long easy ride, variable terrain only if surges remain controlled | Durability/fueling practice; ~38% of cycling time |
| Sun | 45 min | 30 min | Easy cycling + familiar strength, separated if feasible | Low cycling demand; Monday off before Tuesday quality |
| **Total** | **480 min / 8 h** | **60 min** | **540 min / 9 h** | Two hard cycling days, plus strength stress |

<a id="twelve-hour-week"></a>

### 12-hour week

| Day | Cycling | Strength | Objective and approximate intensity / structure | Placement rationale |
|---|---:|---:|---|---|
| Mon | 0 min | 0 | Rest | Protect quality rather than use every day |
| Tue | 90 min | 30 min | VO₂-oriented: 15 warm-up + **5×5** + **4×3** easy + 38 easy finish; strength later | More tolerated work, not evidence that 5×5 is uniquely best |
| Wed | 90 min | 0 | Easy endurance, RPE 2–3 | Adds chiefly easy volume |
| Thu | 90 min | 0 | Sustained work: 15 warm-up + **3×15** + **2×5** easy + 20 easy finish | 45 controlled work minutes; no extra third hard cycling day |
| Fri | 60 min | 0 | Easy endurance/recovery; shorten when needed | Pre-long-ride recovery check |
| Sat | 210 min | 0 | Fueled long easy ride, 3 h 30 | Event-relevant continuous exposure; ~32% of cycling time |
| Sun | 120 min | 30 min | Easy endurance + familiar strength | Adds time only when Tuesday remains intact; Monday off |
| **Total** | **660 min / 11 h** | **60 min** | **720 min / 12 h** | Two hard cycling days; no proportional multiplication of intensity |

Optional event-specific tempo/surges go **instead of** some sustained work or convert the long ride into that week's second hard ride. They are not “free” additions. A VO₂-focused phase may replace Thursday sustained work with a second VO₂ session; preserve the hard-day count.

## A worked twelve-week progression

**Illustration:** a rider already tolerating about seven total hours moves toward nine to nine-and-a-half, retaining one VO₂-oriented and one sustained session. It is not necessary to reach twelve hours to use the twelve-hour availability ceiling. Weekly hours include strength, warm-ups, recoveries and easy riding; interval columns are **work only**, not time near measured VO₂max.

Use the preceding daily pattern, lengthening chiefly easy time to meet each total. Strength: one familiar 30-minute session initially; a second 30-minute session can enter the higher-hour weeks **within** the total, holding other work if soreness appears. New strength development warrants a lower cycling dose. The table is an upper plan contingent on response, not a demand to hit every row.

| Week | Phase / total time | VO₂-oriented exposure | VO₂ work min | Sustained exposure | Sustained work min | Long ride | Recovery emphasis |
|---|---|---|---:|---|---:|---|---|
| 1 | Establish / **7.0 h** | 4×4 | 16 | 2×12 | 24 | 2 h | Find repeatable pace; similar to the 7 h pattern |
| 2 | Establish / **7.5 h** | 5×4 | 20 | 2×15 | 30 | 2 h 15 | Add only if week 1 quality/recovery were normal |
| 3 | Accumulate / **8.0 h** | 5×4 | 20 | 3×12 | 36 | 2 h 30 | Hold VO₂ dose; add chiefly easy time |
| 4 | Reduced-load opportunity / **6.0 h** | Optional 3×3 | 9 | Omit sustained workout | 0 | 1 h 45 | ~25% below prior peak; rest more if actually fatigued |
| 5 | Resume / **8.0 h** | 5×4 | 20 | 3×12 | 36 | 2 h 30 | Return to previously tolerated dose, not a new peak |
| 6 | Accumulate / **8.5 h** | 5×4 | 20 | 2×20 | 40 | 2 h 45 | Longer sustained bouts; do not force more power |
| 7 | Accumulate / **9.0 h** | 5×5 | 25 | 3×15 | 45 | 3 h | Peak only if the preceding dose is repeatable |
| 8 | Reduced-load opportunity / **6.5 h** | Optional 3×3 | 9 | Omit sustained workout | 0 | 2 h | ~28% below prior peak; review performance/recovery |
| 9 | Consolidate / **9.0 h** | 5×4 | 20 | 2×20 | 40 | 3 h | Resume with slightly less hard work |
| 10 | Consolidate / **9.5 h** | 5×5 | 25 | 3×15 | 45 | 3 h 15 | Add easy time; specific late work only as a replacement |
| 11 | Consolidate / **9.5 h** | 5×5 | 25 | 3×15 | 45 | 3 h 15 | Hold rather than demand another increase |
| 12 | Freshen / assess / **6.0 h** | Standardized 5-min field effort | 5 | Standardized 20-min field effort | 20 | 1 h 45 | Tests replace workouts on separate rested days; compare like with like |

The week-12 tests measure field performance, **not VO₂max or automatically FTP**. If using a particular FTP protocol, repeat its entire preparation/test method; do not apply an unqualified 0.95 conversion to any 20-minute effort. Standardize device, cooling, feeding, time of day and preceding recovery. Skip/rearrange tests if unwell or persistently fatigued.

### Adjustment rules and deload basis

- **Hard seven-hour ceiling:** cap total at seven, keep a tolerable quality dose and reduce easy/long duration; the table's later hours are not required. Do not stuff all the later work into fewer minutes by making easy rides hard.
- **Already tolerating ten–twelve hours:** no need to drop to seven first. Apply the same logic to the current baseline; do not multiply hard work proportionately. The twelve-hour example is a separate available-time template.
- **Progression gate:** successful execution and normal recovery, not merely completion. Hold a week, lower power/work or replace a session with easy/rest if several days deteriorate; check fueling, sleep, illness and strength soreness.
- **Calendar deloads:** weeks 4/8 are a convenient coaching opportunity, not a proven universal 3:1 law. Reduce easy duration and accumulated hard work; short intensity is optional, not compulsory during fatigue.
- **Taper versus deload:** pre-event taper has a clearer evidence base (e.g. [Bosquet 2007](https://doi.org/10.1249/mss.0b013e31806010e0), mixed competitive athletes) than routine every-fourth-week recovery. A taper finding does not validate these exact amateur deload percentages.
- **No catch-up:** missed hard work is not banked debt. The goals are sufficient stimulus over weeks and repeatable performance, not weekly arithmetic at the expense of recovery.
