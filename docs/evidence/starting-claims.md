All transcript attributions remain unconfirmed. The [source-tracing brief](../topics/vo2-intervals.md#source-tracing) records candidate confidence, rejected matches and access limits.

The directly retrieved mechanism paper is **Rønnestad & Hansen, journal issue 2016** (an earlier citation/DOI date was 2013). Thirteen well-trained men (VO₂peak 66.7±6.1) performed acute intervals to exhaustion: 30/15 accumulated 678±270 seconds ≥90% VO₂peak versus 397±156 and 366±127 seconds for longer bouts at 50% and 80% of the time-to-exhaustion at maximal aerobic power. The authors explicitly said training adaptation remained untested. This explains the mechanism citation in the 2015 training paper without turning an acute test into longitudinal proof. [Original DOI](https://doi.org/10.1519/JSC.0b013e3182a73e8a); [verified source record](bibliography.md#int54).

### Claim appraisal

| Prompt-described claim | Supported kernel | Oversimplification / uncertainty |
|---|---|---|
| “More fractional VO₂max produces more adaptation” | Odden observed an association | Causality, independent prediction and field measurement unproved |
| “30/15 is better” | Specific small trained/elite comparisons favor selected outcomes | Comparator/prescription matter; neither every performance endpoint nor VO₂max always wins |
| “4–8 minutes is the sweet spot” | Seiler's recreational 4×8 arm performed well | No unique trained-cyclist optimum; work dose and baseline differ |
| “Mesocycle order matters little” | Sylta final 12-week outcomes converged | Early gains differed; not proof all durations/blocks equivalent |
| “~60% FTP is appropriate at 30 hours” | Lower easy intensity can protect recoverability | No direct universal 60-versus-75 adaptation law; FTP does not define LT1 |
| “Four–five easy hours between hard days add little fatigue” | A professional may tolerate it | Substantial work/energy/thermal exposure; not free amateur recovery |
| “Sugar water matches expensive products” | Sucrose provides glucose/fructose; glucose+sucrose mixtures have oxidation comparisons, and sucrose alone has liver-glycogen comparisons | Whole-product performance/GI/sodium/fluid/dental equivalence not shown |
| “Nine hours plus naps is needed” | Sleep extension/naps can help some acute outcomes | No fixed volume-to-sleep law or demonstrated chronic adaptation optimum |
| “Sauna improves blood volume” | Plasma often expands; some longer protocols increase Hb mass | Plasma, Hb concentration, Hb mass, EPO and cool performance are not the same claim |
| “Activation is not strength” | Low-load drills differ from progressive heavy loading | This does not prove every individualized mobility/rehab exercise is useless |
